# How to read for these notes
Reading in passes saves time:

- Abstract and conclusion: fill in the summary.
- Figures and tables: most of the key results are here.
- Methods, targeted: only the parts relevant to your decisions, as set out in the reading list.

# Habits that will pay off later
- Write in your own words. Then the notes can feed straight into your paper without a risk of copying. If you do note an exact phrase, put it in quotation marks with the page number so you never mix it up with your own wording.

- Always record page or table numbers. It makes checking claims quick when you write the paper or get asked at a lab review.

- Keep a separate synthesis file (references/notes/synthesis.md) with one comparison table across all papers: dataset, sample size, method, best result, key limitation. The brief says the related work section should integrate sources rather than list them, and this table shows you the patterns, e.g. “several studies found per-student baselines hard to beat.”

- Note your own critique. “Limitations noticed by me” is the section that shows the critical evaluation the rubric rewards.

- Commit after each paper with a message like “Add reading notes for Adler et al. (2022).”
